## Independent derivations and computations

The expected answers were derived from displayed polynomial identities,
critical equations, sum-of-squares arguments, sign witnesses, and exact
rational coordinate changes. They were not generated by calling the
classifier. Independent computations then used SymPy 1.14.0 to reconstruct
each polynomial from its rational term list and check:

1. Both first derivatives vanish at the origin and the Hessian has rank one,
   for all 22 cases.
2. The stated rational shear, or coordinate swap when needed, gives positive
   normalized transverse second derivative.
3. Exact formal solution of the normalized critical equation reproduces all
   17 finite expected residual orders and their leading coefficients.
4. Each of the five zero-residual cases has zero series through degree 10.
   Its identity as a zero analytic germ is established separately by the
   explicit square and critical-branch arguments in EXAMPLES.md; finite series
   agreement alone is not used as the proof in this example review.

The independent coefficient calculation uses the linear coefficient
\(h=P_{YY}(0)\ne0\): having determined lower coefficients of \(\phi\), its
coefficient at order \(k\) is minus the order-\(k\) coefficient of
\(P_Y(X,\phi)\), divided by \(h\). All expressions remain rational. No
classifier code was imported. The observed summary was:

## Adversarial points specifically reviewed

- Negating the quartic minimum gives a strict maximum; negating its saddle
  variant remains a saddle. Expected coefficients are for the normalized
  polynomial, while classifications concern the original polynomial.
- The branch of \((y+y^2-x^2)^2\) is algebraic and has an infinite power series;
  its residual still vanishes identically.
- The mixed Hessian examples require the exact rational shear
  \(y=Y-2X/3\); the swapped examples have original \(f_{yy}(0)=0\).
- The shifted critical value 7 is subtracted, and the negative coefficient
  \(-10^{-100}\) remains nonzero in rational arithmetic.
- Residual order can exceed degree: the quartic cases have order eight, and
  the degree-six delayed cases have orders twelve and nine.
- The restriction to the Hessian kernel is not the critical residual:
  \(y^2+2x^2y\) vanishes on \(y=0\), but its residual is \(-x^4\).
- For \((1+x)^2(y^2+x^3)\), exact symbolic differentiation confirms the common
  gradient factor \(1+x\) and the distant critical line \(x=-1\). At the
  origin the gradient ideal is \((y,x^2)\), so the local complex critical
  point is isolated with Milnor number 2. Its residual is
  \(x^3+2x^4+x^5\), consistent with order minus one. Direct opposite signs on
  \(y=0\) confirm the saddle independently. Global isolation is not assumed.

No incorrect expected answers or normalization discrepancies remained in the
reviewed files. This finite collection does not establish implementation

to arbitrary smooth flat functions.
