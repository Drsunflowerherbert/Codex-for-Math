## Scope and verdict

This is a source-level audit of the theorem statements and their underlying definitions at repository commit **65f2b33a7309de661a8500e4642e5609a537a8ff**, compared with the exact supplied manuscript **arXiv:2609.37695v3, 13 October 2026**, SHA-256 **3c8287bf41d153c7f0d0950b8a4b477546229333ee66ba7e9ae60e6a387920c6**. Repository root below: `extension/formalisation/`.

**The geometric and dynamical definitions inspected are faithful to the ordinary mathematical statements. I found no semantic weakening of the torus/conjugacy conclusions, no replacement of complex codimension one by real codimension one, and no hidden transitivity or holomorphic-stable-bundle assumption in the statement of Theorem 1.1.**

There is one explicit qualification: the formal statements of **Theorem A, Theorem 1.1, and Theorem 1.3 are conditional on `HomotopyTorusRigidity`**. Theorem 1.2 does not take that premise. The premise is a concrete general topological theorem schema, not a vacuous predicate and not the paper's conclusion renamed. It is unproved in the inspected interface. Thus the precise statement supported by these target files is a conditional formalization relative to this topological input. This is a limit on the manuscript's unqualified formalization claim (pp. 4–5), not evidence of a mathematical error in the manuscript: the paper itself uses the corresponding classical homotopy-torus recognition input through Ghys's Proposition 2.9 argument.

No Lean compilation, installation, setup, or execution was performed in this semantic track. The source reading does not establish that the proof terms compile or that their full dependency closure contains only permitted axioms.

## The explicit HS premise, precisely

The actual definition is `Topology/HomotopyTorusRigidity.lean:33–41` (copied in `Comparator/Challenge.lean:405–412`):

> For every n≥5 and every type M equipped with a fixed topology and Euclidean n-dimensional atlas, if M is a closed smooth real manifold and is topologically homotopy equivalent to the canonical real n-torus, then M is homeomorphic to that torus.

`ClosedSmoothRealManifold` contains compactness, Hausdorffness, second countability, and the smooth Euclidean atlas (`Topology/ClosedSmoothRealManifold.lean:34–41`). It does not falsely define “closed” as merely an arbitrary set. Boundarylessness follows from the whole-Euclidean-space model. Its missing separate connectedness/orientation fields are harmless: a homotopy equivalence with the connected torus implies connectedness, and orientability transfers in this setting. The `ContinuousMap.HomotopyEquiv` input is a genuine pair of continuous maps with both composites homotopic to identity; it is not a homeomorphism disguised as an input.

The realification manifold structures and the homotopy-equivalence/homeomorphism witnesses all use the **same selected topology and atlas**. I found no quantifier switch allowing the proof to change M's topology before invoking the premise.

Its actual application can be located at `Topology/RealTorusHomeomorphOfAffineCover.lean:25–51`: after obtaining a contractible Euclidean cover and a faithful additive complex representation of the fundamental group, the proof obtains a homotopy equivalence at lines 48–49, packages the original closed smooth structure at line 50, then invokes `HS (2 * d) ... M hclosed hequiv` at line 51. The branch assumes d≥3, so only real dimensions 2d≥6 are used there. The surface branch of Theorem 1.1 does not use HS (`Targets/Theorem11.lean:1733–1735`). Theorem 1.3 carries HS into construction of its invariant holomorphic 1-form (`Targets/Theorem13.lean:535`); Theorem A passes HS to 1.1 and 1.3 (`Targets/TheoremA.lean:40–41`).

Consequently:

- **Confirmed scope qualification:** A, 1.1, and 1.3 are not standalone unconditional Lean theorems in the inspected statements; they require an explicit topological theorem witness. In ordinary logical form their result is `HomotopyTorusRigidity → paper conclusion`.
- **Not a demonstrated mathematical defect:** The premise is the classical topological recognition input used in the paper's source chain, not a new dynamical assumption such as transitivity or the desired toral conjugacy itself.
- **Not a hidden axiom declaration:** This file declares a `def ... : Prop`; the main results quantify over a proof of it. That still leaves a hypothesis to discharge before obtaining the unconditional paper statements.
- **Not audited here:** Whether another module supplies a proof of HS, whether all imports check in Lean, and whether the exact allowed-axiom policy is satisfied in the compiled solution. Source search found this interface and its applications; this track did not build an environment or run an axiom checker.

## Edge cases and attempted semantic escape checks

1. **Empty X:** `IsCompactComplexManifold` includes `ConnectedSpace`; standard Mathlib connectedness includes nonemptiness. No empty-manifold countermodel was found.
2. **Dimension zero:** Theorem 1.2 explicitly handles d=0 (`Targets/Theorem12.lean:94–121`), giving the point torus. This is an innocuous broader convention. The rank-one premises in A/1.1/1.3 prevent a zero-dimensional vacuity.
3. **Dimension one/full stable or unstable space:** The Anosov datum itself does not syntactically insist both summands have positive dimension. For compact positive-dimensional diffeomorphisms full contraction or expansion is impossible. Theorem A proves d≥2 from its assumptions. I found no assumption replacing that argument with a hidden dimension restriction.
4. **Unknown real torus dimension in 1.2:** `hT` existentially quantifies n, as the paper's wording does. The proof derives n=2d using invariance of Euclidean open dimension (`Targets/Theorem12.lean:122–161`), rather than assuming it silently.
5. **Hyperbolicity omitted in formal 1.2:** This matches the printed 1.2 statement. The final A target separately proves the complex spectrum is hyperbolic (`Targets/TheoremA.lean:44–46`).
6. **Every datum versus existence of a datum:** The formal statements quantify over an actual `a : AnosovDatum f`. Ordinary “f is Anosov” provides such a witness, and the final conclusions hold for every witness satisfying the stated rank assumptions. This does not weaken the result.
7. **Holomorphicity as an input to 1.1:** Not present. The only geometric rank input is `HasComplexRankOne`; the local-frame holomorphic-subspace predicate is a different definition and is absent from that final statement.
8. **Changed manifold topology at the conclusion:** Not found. Both the domain biholomorphism and real topological homeomorphism refer to the original explicit typeclass topology/atlas on X.
