# Primary source notes

All links below were opened and their relevant statements checked on **2026-10-13 (UTC)**. These are references for standard finite-dimensional linear and convex analysis. The support reductions and finite enumeration arguments in this study are applications developed in the study, not quotations of a new theorem or claims of novelty. No source PDF, source figure, or extended source passage is redistributed here.

Page numbers below are **one-based PDF page numbers**; slide identifiers are given separately when useful.

## 1. Exact alternatives and rational arithmetic

KC Border, *Alternative Linear Inequalities*, September 2013, revision 2020-10-15, author-hosted Caltech notes. [Direct PDF](https://kcborder.caltech.edu/Notes/Alternative.pdf).

- **Proposition 35, p. 17:** for a real matrix and right-hand side, the unrestricted-variable system `Ax <= b` and the multiplier system `pA = 0`, `p b = -1`, `p >= 0` are exact alternatives. This supplies the normalized contradiction certificate after a consistent equality system has been eliminated.
- **Theorems 3–5, p. 3:** rational, rational/integer, and real/rational/integer versions of the Fredholm alternative for `Ax = b`. These cover the separate case of inconsistent equalities.
- **Theorems 7–9, pp. 4–5:** rational and integer versions of the nonnegative-solution Farkas alternative. The study's additional observation that an independent rational support has rational multipliers follows directly by exact linear algebra.

Border's `p > 0` denotes a nonnegative, nonzero vector, not a vector with every coordinate strictly positive (see p. 2 and p. 18). Proposition 35 already uses the unambiguous `p >= 0` normalization.

## 2. Conic support reduction

Dimitri P. Bertsekas, MIT 6.253, *Convex Analysis and Optimization*, Spring 2012, **Lecture 3**. [Direct PDF](https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/6-253-convex-analysis-and-optimization-spring-2012/3e2a100d7fa327637ed8e31771a22c18_MIT6_253S12_lec03.pdf).

- **Carathéodory's theorem, p. 8, part (a); proof, p. 9:** a nonzero vector in a generated cone can be expressed as a positive combination of linearly independent generators, hence with at most the ambient dimension many generators. Applying the theorem in the generators' span gives the rank version.
- **Part (b), p. 8:** the corresponding convex-hull bound is ambient dimension plus one. This is a different statement from the conic bound.

For this study the lifted contradiction target `(0,-1)` is nonzero, so the hypothesis of part (a) applies directly. A zero target can have the empty conic representation; a required nonzero positive dependence is a different problem and must not be assigned the same support bound without another argument.

## 3. Finite-value LP duality and active constraints

Dimitri P. Bertsekas, MIT 6.253, *Convex Analysis and Optimization*, Spring 2012, **Lecture 10**. [Direct PDF](https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/6-253-convex-analysis-and-optimization-spring-2012/9f80ea2051c10cc0f8cf3839983c381a_MIT6_253S12_lec10.pdf).

- **Linear Farkas lemma, p. 7:** conic membership is equivalent to validity of the corresponding homogeneous linear implication.
- **Linear Programming Duality Theorem, p. 8, part (a):** a finite optimal value on either side gives equality of primal and dual values and attained optima on both sides.
- **Proof continued, p. 9:** an optimal objective normal is generated by normals of active primal inequalities; inactive multipliers can be zero.

The displayed primal uses `a_j^T x >= b_j` and minimization. The study uses `<=` and maximization, obtained by changing signs. Combining the active-normal statement with the preceding conic theorem yields the study's support bound for a valid implication over a **nonempty** polyhedron. Feasibility is essential to that argument.

## 4. Eliminating consistent equalities

Stephen Boyd and Lieven Vandenberghe, *Convex Optimization*, author-hosted lecture slides, revised by Stephen Boyd, Lieven Vandenberghe, and Parth Nobel. [Direct PDF](https://web.stanford.edu/~boyd/cvxbook/bv_cvxslides.pdf); [author book and slide page](https://stanford.edu/~boyd/cvxbook/).

- **Eliminating equality constraints, p. 122, slide 4.35:** replace a consistent affine equality set by a parametrization `x = x0 + Fz` and substitute that expression into the remaining problem.
- **Eliminating equality constraints, p. 331:** specify a particular solution and a full-rank matrix whose range is the equality nullspace. The displayed dimension `n-p` presumes independent equality rows; the study instead uses `n-rank(E)` to permit dependent rows.

The study reconstructs equality multipliers by solving a row-space equation after finding the nonnegative inequality multipliers. That reconstruction and its right-hand-side identity are elementary linear-algebra consequences of the parametrization, not a separately attributed result from these slides.

## Limits of these citations

These references establish certificate existence, exact alternatives, conic support reduction, and the equality parametrization. They do **not** by themselves establish that a particular truncated support search, coefficient grid, floating-point computation, or implementation is complete. The study states and proves its finite-search claims separately and distinguishes those claims from exact checker soundness and empirical benchmark outcomes. Ordinary proofs and rational program checks here are not Lean or other proof-assistant formal verification.
