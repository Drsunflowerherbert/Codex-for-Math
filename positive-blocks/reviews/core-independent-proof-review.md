# Independent review of the core positive-block proofs

Reviewed on 2026-10-11 UTC. **Mathematical verdict: accepted, with no required corrections**, for the `Gram.lean`, `Blocks.lean`, and `Corollaries.lean` source fingerprints below. This review covers the exact deficits, sharp inequalities, Gram equality criteria, zero cases, and compression results in these modules.

I authored `Basic.lean` earlier in this project. I did not author or edit the three modules reviewed here: `Gram.lean` and `Blocks.lean` were written by other contributors, and `Corollaries.lean` by another contributor. This is an independent review of those modules, relying on the compiled definitions and proved lemmas in my `Basic.lean`; it is not an independent authorship review of `Basic.lean` itself. The separate final-source reviewer is responsible for independent review of the complete delivered source.

The reference is the supplied `docs/study-study.md`, especially PB1, PB2, the Gram/deficit portions of PB3, and PB5. I inspected the theorem statements and proof terms at source level, checked their algebra against the study, and compiled separate API and degenerate-case checks. No production source was changed during this review.

1. **Quantities and dimensions.** `hsSq M` is the real part of `trace (Mᴴ * M)`, proved in `Basic.lean` to equal the sum of the entrywise complex squared norms. `hsNorm M` is its nonnegative square root. This is the unnormalized Hilbert–Schmidt quantity. The Gram factors have types `X : Matrix r m ℂ` and `Y : Matrix r n ℂ`: the two column types can differ, and the common row type is arbitrary. All relevant types need only be finite. Empty dimensions and singular matrices are included.

2. **Exact sum identity.** `gram_sum_gap` expands `hsSq (X * Xᴴ - Y * Yᴴ)` through `hsSq_sub`, the two Gram trace-cycling identities, and `hsInner_gram`. Writing `a = hsNorm (Xᴴ * X)`, `c = hsNorm (Yᴴ * Y)`, and `β = hsSq (Xᴴ * Y)`, this is precisely `a² + c² - 2β = hsSq (P - Q)`. Nonnegativity gives `gram_sum_le`, and the zero characterization of `hsSq` gives `gram_sum_eq_iff`. Neither commutation of `P` and `Q` nor rank assumptions enter the argument.

3. **Weighted product deficit and inequality.** The left side of `gram_product_deficit` expands as `c²a² + a²c² - 2caβ`, which is exactly `2ac(ac - β)`. This identity is valid even when one norm vanishes. In `gram_product_le`, the zero-norm branches first prove the corresponding Gram factor is zero; then the cross block is zero. Only after these branches does the proof derive `a > 0`, `c > 0`, and `ac > 0`, and use nonnegativity of the weighted squared distance to conclude `β ≤ ac`. No nonzero assumption is omitted in cancellation or hidden in the theorem statement.

4. **Complete Gram equality criterion.** `gram_product_eq_iff` uses the same explicit zero branches. If a diagonal norm vanishes, both product equality and the weighted condition `c • P = a • Q` hold. For positive norms, the weighted deficit is zero exactly when the product gap is zero, and `hsSq_eq_zero_iff` turns the vanishing distance into the matrix equality. `gram_product_eq_iff_normalized` adds both positive-norm hypotheses before dividing; its orientation is `P/a = Q/c`, equivalently `Q = (c/a)P`, as in the study.

5. **Factorization and block wrappers.** `exists_gram_of_posSemidef_fromBlocks` assumes positivity of the full matrix `fromBlocks A B Bᴴ C` and uses its positive square root to construct factors with common row type `m ⊕ n`. The three submatrix identities recover exactly `A = Xᴴ * X`, `B = Xᴴ * Y`, and `C = Yᴴ * Y`. The converse `posSemidef_fromBlocks_iff_exists_gram` reconstructs the full Gram matrix. The inequalities, exact deficits, and norm-notation wrappers transport these identities directly. The existential equality criteria include the actual three factorization equations, so their witnesses are constrained to the original blocks.

6. **Every Gram factorization and zero cases.** The `*_of_gram` theorems quantify over an arbitrary finite row type `r`, rather than only the square-root construction. Thus their equality criteria hold in every common Gram factorization, including ones with redundant rows. Separate Lean checks compose these equivalences between unrelated finite row types. I also checked that a zero left diagonal block gives product equality with an arbitrary remaining block, whereas sum equality in that case forces the remaining block to vanish. The symmetric right-zero case is covered by the corresponding proved off-diagonal lemma.

7. **Normalized deficit and comparison of equality cases.** `gram_normalized_product_deficit` in `Corollaries.lean` is exactly study equation (5), `ac - β = (ac/2) hsSq (P/a - Q/c)`, under `a > 0` and `c > 0`. Its real-scalar expansion and denominator clearing are valid with those hypotheses. `hs_sum_deficit_decomposition` proves `a² + c² - 2β = (a-c)² + 2(ac-β)`. Finally, `block_sum_eq_iff_product_eq_and_norms_eq` uses the product inequality to show that sum equality is equivalent to product equality together with `a = c`, including all zero cases.

8. **Compression.** `fromBlocks_compression` correctly identifies the simultaneous compression block matrix with `[U V]ᴴ S [U V]`, using Hermitian symmetry of `S` for the lower-left block. Positivity follows by congruence, and both compression inequalities follow by the proved block inequalities. `exists_compression_gram` additionally constructs the exact factors `sqrt(S) * U` and `sqrt(S) * V` with row type `r`. Their factorization equations allow the every-Gram equality criteria to be applied to these factors as in PB5. The factors `U` and `V` may be arbitrary rectangular matrices.
